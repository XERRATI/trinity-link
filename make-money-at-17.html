Digital Skills You Can Start at 17

By Trinity · 21 October 2026 · 6 minute read

This page is honest about two things most people are not: what a teenager can realistically do first, and what you legally need an adult for. No income promises. The order below is the one that avoids wasting a year.

First, the boring part that nobody mentions

Under 18, most payment processors will not pay you directly. Stripe requires an adult account holder. Payhip allows 13–17 with parental consent. That means before any money can reach you, a parent or guardian has to be on the account. That is not a workaround to find — it is a step to do, once, with them.

Get that done in week one. Not because you are ready to sell, but because the alternative is making something good and then discovering you cannot take payment for it.

The order that works

1. Learn a skill for 30 days

Two hours a day, practice only. Pick one: video editing, design, copywriting, simple automation, or AI-assisted services. Do not buy a course. There is more free material on any of these than you could finish in a year.

2. Build proof for 30 days

Five to ten real pieces. Your own channel counts. So does work you did free for someone. What matters is that a stranger can look at it and tell what you can do.

3. Sell the skill before you sell a product

This is the step almost everyone skips. A first job — even a small one at a low price — teaches you pricing, deadlines, and how to talk to someone who is paying. That experience is worth more than the money.

4. Only then, a product

A digital product needs an audience or it needs advertising, and you have neither at the start. Selling your time first is faster and requires no audience at all.

What not to do: do not buy a "done for you" course or a resell-rights product as your first move. You would be paying for someone else's work and competing with everyone else who bought the same thing.

How much time this actually takes

Two focused hours a day, six days a week. That is roughly seventy hours a month — about a full week of school. In three months you can be genuinely competent at one skill. Not expert. Competent enough to be useful, which is what gets you the first job.

What the first money looks like

Usually small and unglamorous: one job, badly paid, for someone who took a chance on you. That is normal and it is the correct first step. Two things are true at once — some people land their first paid job in a couple of months, and many do not. Nobody can give you a date, and anyone who does is selling something.

The three habits that decide it

  • Same wake time. Everything else is built on it.
  • Two hours before the internet gets you. Whatever hour you do not protect, the feed takes.
  • Keep the promise to yourself once. Miss once, not twice. That is the whole system of discipline, and it is the real requirement here — more than talent.

Frequently asked

Can a 17 year old sell digital products online?

Yes, but you need a parent or guardian on the payment account. Stripe requires an adult account holder, and Payhip permits sellers aged 13 to 17 only with parental consent. Set that up before you build anything.

What skill should I learn first at 17?

Video editing or design, because results are visible quickly and small businesses need both constantly. Copywriting pays more per job but takes longer before your work looks good.

Do I need a big following to earn?

No. Selling a skill to a specific person requires no audience at all. An audience matters for selling a product, which is a later step.

How long until I make money?

It varies too much for an honest number. What is consistent is the order: practice, proof, first small paid job, then product. Skipping straight to a product is the most common way this fails.
