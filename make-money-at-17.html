How to Make Money at 17

By Trinity · 21 October 2026 · 7 minute read

Quick answer

At 17, you earn from a skill before you earn from a business. Pick one service skill, do it free for three people to build proof, then charge a small fixed price. Check each platform's age rules first — several require 18, and most payouts need a parent's account or consent.

Two things are true at once. You can earn money at 17 from a skill, and most of what the internet sells you about doing it is either illegal-adjacent, age-blocked, or a lie about the timeline. This page is the boring version that works.

The 90-day version

  1. Days 1–30 — skill. Choose one thing that produces something visible (see the one skill guide). Produce something every day, however small.
  2. Days 31–45 — proof. Do it free for three people, but only with a deadline and a deliverable. Free with no deadline is a favour; free with a deadline is a portfolio.
  3. Days 46–60 — price. Set one small fixed price and say it without apologising. Fixed beats hourly at this stage because you cannot yet estimate hours.
  4. Days 61–90 — repeat. Ask every satisfied person for one introduction. That is the entire marketing plan, and it works better than posting into the void.

The age rules, plainly

  • Payment processors set the floor, not you. Stripe requires account holders to be 18; a parent or guardian has to own the account and the payout.
  • Stores vary. Some allow 13–17 with verified parental consent; some require 18 outright. Check the specific store's terms, in writing, before you build anything in it.
  • Tax doesn't disappear because you're young. Rules differ by country and by amount; ask a parent to check, and keep a simple record of everything you receive from day one.
  • Platform terms are contracts. Using a platform while under its age limit gets the account closed and the money frozen — often after you have done the work.

What is honest to expect

The first payment is usually small and usually comes from someone who already knows you. That is not failure, it is the normal shape of a first sale: proof, then price, then referrals. Anyone quoting you a monthly figure before you have a skill has something to sell you.

What you control in 90 days: the number of reps, the number of people who see the work, and whether you ask for the sale. What you do not control: the result, the timing, or the market.

What not to bother with

  • Dropshipping courses. The course is the product; you are the customer.
  • "Private label" anything at 17. Capital, accounts and logistics all need an adult and a lot of money.
  • Trading, betting, "signals". Not a skill, not an income, and frequently a loss.
  • Buying resell rights to someone else's PDF. You would be paying to become their distribution channel.

Frequently asked

Can I make money at 17?

Yes, from a skill sold directly — writing, editing, building a page, bookkeeping for a local business. The limit is not your age, it is payment infrastructure: most processors require an account holder to be 18, so a parent or guardian usually needs to own the account and receive the payout.

What is the fastest skill to learn at 17?

Video editing and copywriting have the shortest path from zero to a first small job, because the output is immediately visible and the demand is constant. Neither is fast in absolute terms — expect a first paid job in the 60–120 day range, not the first week.

Do I need to register a business?

At the scale of a first few jobs, most places do not require it, but rules vary by country. Ask a parent or a local adviser, and keep records from the first payment.
