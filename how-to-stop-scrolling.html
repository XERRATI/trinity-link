How to Stop Scrolling Your Phone

By Trinity · 21 October 2026 · 6 minute read

Quick answer

You stop scrolling by making the first hour phone-free, giving the feed a stopping cue, and filling the thirty-second gaps that trigger it. Deleting apps fails because it treats a design problem as a willpower problem — and the strongest trial evidence says removing the pathway beats removing the device.

Nobody scrolls for ninety minutes because the content is that good. They scroll because there is a gap and the phone fills it faster than anything else. Close the gaps and the scrolling shrinks. That is the whole method.

The evidence, briefly

The cleanest test is a 2025 randomised trial that blocked mobile internet on 467 adults' phones for two weeks. Daily screen time fell from 314 to 161 minutes — roughly half — and 91% improved on at least one measure of attention, mental health or well-being. They kept their phones. They lost the feed.

Castelo, Kushlev et al., PNAS Nexus, 2025 — 467 adults, two-week randomised mobile internet block, screen time 314 → 161 min/day.

And when 2,743 people deactivated Facebook for four weeks in a 2020 experiment, well-being rose about 0.09 standard deviations and they recovered roughly an hour a day.

Allcott et al., The Welfare Effects of Social Media, American Economic Review, 2020.

The three changes

1. The first hour belongs to you

No phone for the first sixty minutes after you wake. Not airplane mode with a peek — nothing. Water, light, movement. This does more than every blocker combined, because the first hour sets what your attention is worth for the rest of the day. Start with a feed and you will reach for one in every gap until midnight.

2. Give the feed a stopping cue

The feed never ends, so you have to give it an edge. Before you open it, name what you are looking for. When you have it, close it. If you cannot name it, you are not opening it to find something — you are opening it to be filled.

3. Make the gaps expensive

Most scrolling happens in thirty-second gaps: the queue, the lift, the kettle, the walk between rooms. Fill them deliberately — a book in the bag, headphones with something downloaded, a notebook. Anything that takes under five seconds to start beats the phone.

The one-day test

For one day, change nothing and count every time you reach for the phone without a reason. Most men land above fifty. That number is the actual size of the problem, and it is the fastest thing you will ever improve.

What not to bother with

  • Screen time limits. You will tap ignore within a week and then feel bad about it. Guilt is not a strategy.
  • Ten new blocker apps. Managing the blockers becomes the new scrolling.
  • Deleting everything in a burst of motivation. You will reinstall in three days and trust yourself less.
  • Grey-scale. It works for about a week and the effect fades. Fine as a nudge, useless as a plan.

Frequently asked

How do I stop scrolling without deleting apps?

Make the first hour phone-free, name what you are opening the app for and close it when you have it, and fill the short gaps with something that takes under five seconds to start. The 2025 PNAS Nexus trial found cutting the pathway — not the device — halved screen time.

Does screen time limiting work?

Poorly on its own. It reports a number after the fact rather than preventing the behaviour, and most people override it within a week.

Why can't I just use willpower?

Because you are not fighting a habit, you are fighting a design: no end, no stopping cue, a new thing every two seconds. Willpower runs out around the same time every day, which is exactly when the feed is strongest.
